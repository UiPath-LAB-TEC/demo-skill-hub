# Source Policy

Use current web research when account, leadership, initiatives, product lines, regulations, partnerships, or market conditions may have changed.

## Preferred Sources

- Official company websites.
- Annual reports, filings, investor materials, and press releases.
- Government, regulator, payer, provider, industry association, and standards-body sources.
- Product documentation or API documentation for named systems.
- Credible industry publications for market context.

## Citation Rules

- Cite sources for customer-facing facts.
- Use exact dates for time-sensitive information.
- Label inferences and assumptions separately from sourced facts.
- Assign confidence to important claims: High for directly sourced account facts, Medium for well-supported industry inferences, Low for weak or indirect signals.
- Do not rely on unsourced claims for internal systems, volumes, staffing, or process rules.

## Research Boundaries

Research should enrich discovery. It should not invent a customer process when the customer has not described one.

When customer-specific evidence is thin, provide a best-fit process classification, known external facts, and prioritized discovery questions. Mark taxonomy, process, system, and metric assumptions clearly.

Use research to identify likely operational metrics to validate. Do not convert public industry context into customer-specific volume, staffing, SLA, AHT, backlog, quality, revenue, or risk values.
